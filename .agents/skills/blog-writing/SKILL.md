# Blog Writing

Read [references/writing-standard.md](references/writing-standard.md) completely before drafting or editing a post.

Treat Spencer as the author and Codex as an editor and technical collaborator. Preserve his ideas, experiences, judgments, and recognizable cadence. Do not manufacture personal experience, results, citations, data, quotes, or demo behavior.

## Choose the mode

- **Draft:** Work from Spencer's notes, claims, artifacts, or rough prose. Establish the point quickly and organize around concrete evidence rather than an essay-shaped introduction.
- **Proofread:** Make the smallest edits that produce grammatical, spelling, punctuation, and usage correctness. Preserve intentional fragments, contractions, technical vocabulary, pacing, and jokes. Separate factual or structural concerns from copy edits.
- **Publication review:** Audit every major claim, its reader-visible evidence, graphics or demo, accessibility, links, and SEO metadata. Do not declare a post ready while a major claim lacks support.

## Evidence gate

Inventory the post's major claims before finalizing it. A major empirical, performance, causal, comparative, historical, or novelty claim must have credible support. Prefer primary sources and first-party measurements.

When a claim depends on measured or visual behavior, give the reader visible evidence: a labeled plot, diagram, screenshot, recording, or interactive demo, plus enough context to interpret it. Pair demos with source or reproduction details where practical. Do not substitute decorative art for evidence.

If evidence is unavailable, do one of the following instead of inventing it:

- ask Spencer for the missing artifact or source;
- mark a precise TODO in a working draft;
- narrow the claim to what the available evidence establishes; or
- label it clearly as opinion, hypothesis, recollection, or anecdote.

## Publishing invariants

- Blog prose is English-only. Never translate or create localized variants of a post.
- Keep posts in `blogs/spencer/_posts/` and assets in `blogs/spencer/assets/` unless the repository establishes a more specific location.
- Preserve Liquid and Jekyll syntax, code samples, footnotes, citations, and asset paths.
- Require useful alt text for informative images and visible attribution where licensing or provenance warrants it.
- Ensure front matter and SEO are complete using the repository conventions in the writing standard. Do not hand-author canonical, Open Graph, Twitter card, or JSON-LD tags already generated by the shared blog templates.
- Run focused formatting and a Jekyll production build after material edits. Verify the generated title, description, canonical URL, social image, author JSON-LD, and sitemap entry when SEO metadata changed.

Lead the handoff with what changed, then identify unresolved evidence, factual, demo, or SEO issues. Never bury a publication blocker beneath copy-edit notes.
